PocketTerm

Privacy policy

Applies to the PocketTerm apps for Mac, Windows, iPhone and Android published by Danijel Lovric (“the publisher”). Last updated 19 October 2026.

The publisher runs no PocketTerm cloud and never receives what your terminals and coding sessions carry. The apps connect to a server you own. The only thing the publisher operates is a relay that forwards encrypted push notifications to Apple and Google.

Where your data lives

PocketTerm keeps what it needs to connect your devices in a Supabase project that you create and own. The desktop app's setup wizard creates it in your Supabase account, or you set it up with a script. Each app connects only to the server it was set up or paired with. The publisher has no access to it. On that server PocketTerm keeps:

  • your account, from signing in with GitHub: the profile GitHub shares at sign-in, which includes your GitHub user name, display name, avatar URL and email address;
  • your computers: each one's name, whether it is a Mac or a Windows PC, and when it was last online;
  • the codes that sign a phone in, kept only as a hash;
  • your phones' push registrations: the phone's push token, a key the phone generated (below), the phone's name and whether it is an iPhone or an Android phone;
  • which coding sessions each phone follows on its Lock Screen or in its notifications, by the session's terminal window (for example a process and window number), never its title or contents.

What your terminals carry isn't stored. Prompts, the agent's answers and tool calls, questions, terminal screens and keystrokes travel between your computer and your phones over a Realtime channel on your server that only your account can join. Nothing on it is saved. Images you send from the phone to a session are uploaded to a private storage bucket in your project, in a folder only your account can reach, and the computer deletes them once it has fetched them.

If you make reviewer codes (Settings › Reviewer access, so that App Store or Google Play reviewers can try the phone app), they sign a phone into a separate demo account on your server, which sees none of your computers or sessions.

What happens to the data on your server is up to you and Supabase's terms.

On your devices

So that the Terminal tab can show the last known state before your computer answers, the phone app keeps a copy of the sessions it showed in its own app storage. A session's copy that hasn't changed for 14 days is deleted, and uninstalling the app removes all of it. On Android the app's data is excluded from backups and device-to-device transfers, so the push key and your sign-in stay on that phone.

Deleting your account

Choose Delete account in Settings, in the desktop app or the phone app. The images in your storage folder are deleted first, then your account and with it every record of yours on your server: your computers, sign-in codes, push registrations and followed sessions. You can also delete the whole Supabase project in Supabase's dashboard.

What the apps send, and to whom

  • To your server: what is listed above, and, after a wrong sign-in code, the address the attempt came from as Supabase's edge reports it, kept to slow down guessing (entries older than an hour are cleared on the next wrong code).
  • To GitHub: when you sign in on a computer, GitHub's usual sign-in exchange, through the GitHub OAuth app on your server. During setup the wizard also checks that OAuth app's client ID and secret with GitHub. Phones never sign in with GitHub: they sign in with a code from your computer, which your server checks.
  • To Supabase, during setup: the wizard uses a Supabase access token that you paste to create and configure your project through Supabase's Management API. The token and the secrets you enter are kept only while the wizard runs and aren't saved.
  • To the publisher's push relay (phones, when pushes are on):
    • Every notification is encrypted on your server with an AES-256 key that only your phone and your server know. The relay receives the phone's push token, the kind of notification, a collapse id (which computer and terminal window the session runs in, so a newer push replaces an older one) and the ciphertext.
    • It can't read session titles, the agent's messages or terminal output. It forwards the message to Apple (APNs) or Google (Firebase Cloud Messaging) and stores nothing on disk. To keep floods cheap it counts requests per sending address and per push token (hashed) in memory, which goes when the function restarts.
    • One exception, on iPhone: iOS shows Live Activity updates without running the app's code, so those can't be encrypted. For them the relay and Apple see the session's state (working, needs you, finished, ended), since when, and, when it needs you, whether for a question, an approval or a plan, nothing else. Session titles, project names and the question itself never travel in a Live Activity push. On Android a followed session's state is encrypted like every other notification.
    • The relay runs on Supabase, whose platform keeps short-lived request logs (time, status, errors). The relay doesn't log tokens or message contents.
  • To Apple or Google: push notifications are delivered by APNs (iPhone) or Firebase Cloud Messaging (Android), under their own privacy terms. The Android app includes Firebase Cloud Messaging only to receive these messages. It includes no Firebase Analytics or Crashlytics.
  • Updates and downloads: the Mac app checks GitHub Releases for new versions (Sparkle), and the Windows app asks GitHub for the latest release. On Windows, the Install button for WezTerm downloads WezTerm's installer from WezTerm's GitHub release. These requests carry your IP address, as any download does.

The apps contain no analytics, advertising or tracking SDKs, and the publisher doesn't sell or share data, because it doesn't have any.

Permissions

  • Camera (phones): only to scan the setup code your computer shows. Images from the camera aren't stored or sent.
  • Photos (phones): only the images you pick to send to a coding session, uploaded to your server and deleted once your computer has fetched them.
  • Notifications (phones): pushes when a session finishes or needs you, and the sessions you follow.
  • Automation (Mac): to read and type into iTerm2, Terminal and Ghostty windows, if you allow it.
  • Terminal from phone (desktop): while it is on, phones signed in to your account can read your computer's coding sessions (omp, Claude Code) and terminal windows, prompt them, answer their questions, type into them and open or close windows, over your server's Realtime channel. Nothing it carries is stored. It is a switch in Settings.

Children

PocketTerm is not directed at children under 13.

This website

The website uses no advertising trackers or cookies. It counts visits with Umami, a cookieless analytics tool hosted on danyzmaj's own server in Germany: the page viewed, the site you came from, your browser, operating system, device type, screen size, language, and approximate location (country, region and city) derived from your IP address. Your IP address itself isn't stored, nothing is saved on your device, and the data isn't shared or sold. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, which GitHub states logs and stores visitors' IP addresses for security purposes, so hosting itself is not a zero-data service. See GitHub's Privacy Statement.

Support correspondence

If you email support, I receive your email address, your message, and anything you choose to attach. My email provider also processes those messages. I use them to answer questions and fix problems, and I don't add you to a marketing list. Issues you open on GitHub are public and fall under GitHub's terms.

Changes

Changes to this policy are posted on this page with a new date. The apps, the server they set up and the push relay are open source at github.com/danyzmaj-apps/pocketterm, so you can check what they send.
